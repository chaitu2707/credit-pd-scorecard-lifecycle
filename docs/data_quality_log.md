# Data quality log

Generated by `python -m src.data_prep`. Outputs are written to
`reports/tables/data_quality_funnel.csv` and `reports/tables/label_sensitivity.csv`.

## 1. Source

Lending Club accepted loans, 2007 to 2018 Q4. 2,260,701 rows, 1.67 GB.

## 2. Rules applied, in order

| # | Rule | Rows remaining | Rows removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Raw file | 2,260,701 | 0 |
| 1 | Final outcome only: drop Current, In Grace Period, Late (16–30), Late (31–120), Does not meet the credit policy with an open status | 1,348,099 | 912,602 |
| 2 | Issued on or before 2017-09-30 (full 12-month window before the 2018-12-31 cut-off, with a 3-month buffer) | 1,257,680 | 90,419 |
| 3 | Charged-off loans with no last payment date (default timing cannot be estimated) | 1,256,067 | 1,613 |

Rows removed by rule 1 are mostly loans still Current. Their removal is the main source of selection toward older, shorter-term loans in recent vintages. This is reported in the validation report.

## 3. Label

- **Base case:** bad (1) if charged off or defaulted, and the estimated default falls within 12 months of issue. The default date is estimated as last payment date + 120 days.
- **Good (0):** fully paid, or charged off after the 12-month window.

| Measure | Loans |
|---|---|
| Charged off over life | 251,544 |
| Bad within 12 months (target = 1) | 55,777 |
| Charged off after 12 months (relabelled good) | 195,767 |

**Overall 12-month bad rate (base case): 4.44%**

## 4. Lag decision

**Decision:** charge-off lag of 120 days, base case (decided by the model owner).

**Reason:** 120 days gives the higher PD of the three tested lags. For provisioning and capital, overstating PD is the more conservative error, so 120 days is the safer base. Lending Club's charge-off policy is around 120 days past due, but days past due start at the first missed payment, so the true lag from last payment is likely longer (about 150 days). The 150-day case is therefore reported as the lower bound.

**Sensitivity (`reports/tables/label_sensitivity.csv`):**

| Lag (days) | Bad loans | Bad rate, all | Bad rate, training | Bad rate, validation | Bad rate, out-of-time | Loans that change label vs base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | 65,436 | 5.21% | 3.22% | 4.25% | 7.82% | 9,659 |
| **120 (base)** | **55,777** | **4.44%** | **2.75%** | **3.58%** | **6.71%** | **0** |
| 150 | 45,635 | 3.63% | 2.26% | 2.88% | 5.53% | 10,142 |

**Findings:**
- The overall bad rate ranges 3.6% to 5.2% across 90 to 150 days, about 1.6 percentage points. This exceeds the 0.5-point threshold, so the lag is reported as a limitation.
- The direction is the same at every lag: the bad rate rises from training to validation to out-of-time. The 2016–2017 deterioration is not an artefact of the lag. Only its size depends on it.
- About 17% of base-case bad loans sit near the 12-month boundary and change label across the range.

**Open item:** Stage 4 fits the scorecard on the base label and on the 150-day label, and compares out-of-time Gini and KS. If the ranking is stable, the lag affects PD levels, not rank order. This result goes in the validation report.

## 5. Known limitations of the label

- The default date is estimated from the last payment date, not observed.
- Accepted loans only. Rejected applicants are not in the file, so there is no reject inference.
- Recent vintages are dominated by 36-month loans, because 60-month loans issued in 2016–2017 are mostly still Current and were removed by rule 1.
